# Hanabi hint tracker

## The pitch

A phone-sized web app that stands in for the notes you can't write on the back of
your own Hanabi cards. You hold five cards you're not allowed to look at; when a
teammate tells you "these two are 3s", you tap those cards and the hint sticks to
them. Reorder, discard, draw — the app mirrors your real hand.

## Problem

Hanabi asks two things of a player at once: deduce what your cards are, and
remember every hint you've ever been given about them. The second part is pure
bookkeeping, and it's the part that fails first. When it fails, the deduction
built on top of it fails too, and the table slows to a crawl while someone
reconstructs what was said three turns ago.

## Why it matters

In a mixed-age group the memory load lands unevenly. Players who enjoy the
reasoning get shut out of it by the recall, and the game stops being fun for the
people it stopped being easy for. Offloading recall doesn't cheapen Hanabi — the
information is legal information the player already received. It just stops the
game from quietly testing the wrong skill.

## Who it's for

A group playing Hanabi in person around a table, each player holding their own
phone. Primary audience is casual and mixed-age groups rather than convention
players; the design bias is toward large, unambiguous, glanceable cards over
information density.
